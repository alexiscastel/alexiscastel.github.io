This dissertation develops computational models for composite structures with passive damping treatments. A general equivalent-single-layer plate formulation uses transverse shear warping functions to represent the behavior through the thickness. It recovers established plate theories and introduces two approaches for determining the warping functions.

Navier solutions, Rayleigh–Ritz discretization, and finite element formulations are used to study heterogeneous multilayer plates and plates with constrained-layer damping patches. Comparisons with three-dimensional finite element calculations assess the models’ static and dynamic predictions. Energy analysis examines how a damping patch stores and dissipates mechanical energy. The dissertation also couples the mechanical model with a genetic algorithm for inverse identification of viscoelastic material properties.
